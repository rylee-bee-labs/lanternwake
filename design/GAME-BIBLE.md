# LANTERNWAKE — the game bible

A game by **Far Lantern Studio**.
Built on VEFR (the bones); this pack is the flesh.

One line:

> The Long Dim has fallen on the Lanternwake Coast. You are the Guild's
> last apprentice. Re-light the five Great Lanterns, carry the news
> between the harbor towns, and find out what happened the night the
> bell drowned.

Genre: cozy-dark exploration. A walking game with light combat,
letters and lore, and a town full of people who are wrong about
each other. No gore. The horror is forgetting, never blood.

---

## 1. The world

The **Lanternwake Coast** — a headland of salt marsh, moor and black
pine over cold water. Five settlements cling to it, strung along the
old coast road.

For generations the **Guild of the Wake** kept the lamps along that
road: ninety-nine flames from the Beacon at Vesper Point down to the
last candle in a marsh window. The Guild taught one plain thing —
**light is how a place keeps its name**.

Ten years ago the **Long Dim** began. Each year the dusk came earlier
and stood longer. In unlit places the **Hush** pooled: a dark that
does not kill. It forgets. Things left in the Hush lose their names,
then their shapes, then their reasons. People taken by it come back
kind and empty.

Then the equinox storm: the chapel bell drowned, the Guildhall burned,
the Master Lampwright vanished, and the lamps went out one by one.
The coast has been keeping its own small lights since — candles,
hearths, hooded lanterns — and losing ground.

Now the Guild's last apprentice arrives at Thornhollow with two
heirlooms: the **Evercan** (the Guild's ever-full oil can) and the
**ledger**.

### The creed

> Light is how a place keeps its name.

### Tone rules (the voice of every line)

- Plain words. Short sentences. Sea-weather vocabulary.
- Kindness shown as work: a lit lamp, a mended rope, a kept seat.
- Never explain the Hush. Show what it does to ordinary things.
- No modern words. No jokes at anyone's expense.
- Quiet dread, warm hands. Nothing gory, nothing cruel.

## 2. The truth of the backstory (the author's canon)

The mystery has one true shape. What people *believe* is scattered;
the game is gathering it.

- **Master Lampwright Corwin Thale** discovered the Great Lantern
  under the Drowned Chapel — the first lamp of the coast — failing.
- **Osric Vane**, Warden of the Drowned Chapel (brother of Captain
  Orra Vane of Gullwatch), came to believe the Hush was judgment and
  the lamps were pride. The night of the equinox he cut the bell down
  and let the sea take it, "to end the false light".
- Corwin stopped him too late to save the bell, and went down into
  the Hush after it. The Hush took his name. He is alive — the
  nameless keeper who lights candles along the deep road, one a night,
  for a reason he can no longer say.
- Osric survived, mad with guilt, and let the story be told as
  *"the Master fled"*. He walks the drowned nave still, waiting to be
  forgiven or taken.
- The Guildhall fire was accident — a storm lantern and a dry beam.
  The burning scattered the Guild; nothing more.

No villain is a monster here. The Warden is a grieving man who was
wrong. The Master is a hero who paid in the only coin he had.

## 3. The structure — five lights and a beacon

The spine is six lit places. Each is a `uses-with` beat with the
Evercan at a named lamp POI, plus its own region content.

| # | The light | Where | What it costs the player |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Causeway Lamp** | Toll Rock, mid-causeway | the tutorial light; teaches the Evercan |
| 2 | **The Ferry Lamp** | Saltreach ferry slip | marsh walk; salt-wights |
| 3 | **The Cliff Lamp** | Gullwatch headland | the cliff path; the first big climb |
| 4 | **The Weald Lamp** | the Ash Clearing in the Weald | the dark wood; the hermit's price |
| 5 | **The Chapel Lamp** | the drowned nave | the deep road; the Warden |
| 6 | **the Beacon** | Vesper Point | the finale: all five, the bell rope, the Master |

Chapters (see `CONTENT-PLAN.md` for the hours ledger):

1. **The Hollow** — Thornhollow and its cellars. Learn the verbs,
   meet the town, light the Causeway Lamp. (~6h)
2. **The Brine Crossing** — the causeway, Saltreach, the marsh.
   The Ferry Lamp. (~7h)
3. **The Boneyard Cliffs** — Gullwatch, the whale beach, the Cliff
   Lamp. (~7h)
4. **The Deep Road** — the Weald, Barrow Mouth, the Under-Stair.
   The Weald Lamp. (~8h)
5. **The Drowned Chapel** — the nave, the Warden, the Chapel Lamp,
   the Beacon. (~7h)
6. **The Long Tail** — every book, every relic, every corrected
   belief, every light. (~5h)

## 6. The systems map (engine → game)

| VEFR system | What it is in Lanternwake |
|---|---|
| regions + transitions | the coast road: 13 hand-built regions + 8 deep floors |
| fog + light items | the Hush in wild places; the Evercan, torches, storm lanterns |
| speakers + voices + fragments | 30 named people, heard with no model |
| phases (4) | the watches of one long night: dusk · moonrise · deep-night · first-light |
| `water_by_phase` + `flood_tiles` | the tide: the causeway and the nave flood at deep-night |
| whispers (Q) | what the coast is saying right now (grammar + banks) |
| library books | the Guild's paper trail — 50 books, read offline |
| items + bag + gold + shops | trade goods, food, tools; two shops and one stall |
| enemies + combat | the Hush's husks and things; Cozy death wakes you at the inn |
| rules (≤40) | the six lights, the evidence, the corrected beliefs, the weather |
| grammars | offline whispers, weather, and names for the deep floors |
| `norns delve` | the Under-Stair and the Salt Cellars, generated then baked |
| the ledger / logbok | the canon every generated line obeys |

## 7. What the game refuses

- No fail states. Death is Cozy; a wrong belief is a journal line.
- No timers, no pressure, no scores. The tide is a mood, not a threat.
- No gore. The Hush takes names, not blood.
- No puzzle that blocks the spine: every Great Lantern is reachable
  by walking; the mystery deepens play, it never gates it.

## 8. Play surface

`surface: "combat"` — the HP bar is honest but gentle (costume floor;
the player never drops below one... the engine's Cozy death restores
to full and wakes the hero). `verbs: ["attack", "soothe"]`: Attack
bites, Soothe is a spoken line — both are roleplay first.
