First night out on the road alone. The Master said, keep to the shingle and mind the wick. I held the pitch torch in two hands and it guttered at every gust, so I walked bent over it like a man cupping a newborn. Past the second lamp-post the road went black on both sides. I counted my own breaths to the bend. Twelve. Sable says sing on a night like that. I could not get a sound up past my teeth, and the dark sat there and let me.

* * *

Somewhere after the bend the torch stopped fighting me. The flame stood up straight and gave out a proper ring of light, and in it a moth, grey as ash, riding the warmth the way a leaf rides a tide. Nothing came out of the dark. The dark only sat at the edge of it, the way a dog sits at the edge of a yard and waits to be told. I walked the rest easy. Eight lamps checked, every one of them lit. Home by moonrise. I have written the whole of it down so I keep the part before.
