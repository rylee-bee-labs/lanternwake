title: The Physician's Counts

Counted at dusk, eleventh year of the Dim.

Lamps in the hollow: nine standing, nine lit. Wicks trimmed by a hand that was not mine: four.

Patients since the frost: twelve. Fever, four. A child's cut on shingle, one. Old bones against the causeway steps, two. The rest is worry, which comes in with the same signs as fever and will not answer to anything in my bottle.

I write the numbers down so the night has somewhere to end.

* * *

Days since the bell: three thousand six hundred and some. I do not count the leap day. It does not care whether I count it.

Coast road lamps, last count sixty-one, this count fifty-eight. The three that went dark were not taken by anything. They were unkept. There is a difference and I mean to keep it.

The Master: gone the same night. No body on the shingle, no letter at the inn, no hand at any door in ten years.

What has no count does not stop being true. It only stops being argued with.

* * *

The rule of the counts: a body is a body. A lamp is a lamp. A man who cannot be found is not a man who has ceased.

Write it plainly:

the Master is dead

Twice struck through this morning, hard enough to go into the paper. I have not put a line in its place. When I hold a count that fits, I will write it there. Until then the page stays as it is, which is honest, which is more than that line ever was.
