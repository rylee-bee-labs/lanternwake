The hymn I sing the bell at dusk. Written down because the Warden said a chorister who cannot write his music is only a noise with a collar.

(The page has been wet and dried so many times it has rings like a cup, and the pen pulls where the paper is proud.)

BELL OF THE BAY, BELL OF THE STONE,
RING US HOME WHEN THE LIGHT IS GONE.
SMALL HANDS ON THE ROPE, SMALL WORDS TO SAY,
BELL, WE ARE HERE, BELL, WE ARE DAY.

I keep it by the water's edge. The sea has taken the corners and I paste them back with whatever is to hand.

* * *

THE SECOND VERSE, WHICH IS THE LAST:

BELL OF THE BAY, BELL UNDER THE WEATHER,
WE WILL COME DOWN AND SING TO YOU —

and then —

Sing to ... the ink runs out where the water sat, and past that the lines are only where the pen started and did not go.

That is the night the bell answered. Not a word. One big note, under me and over me, me with my mouth open and my pen down.

I have never finished the verse. When they ask why, I say the bell finished it.

— Sef, in the drowned nave, whenever dusk is.
