CROSSINGS OWED — slate kept in the toll booth, chalk, and the rain gets at it twice a day at high water.

Marta Brine, cart and horse, salt and oil — owes one crossing. Paid in talk.
Quill the clerk, papers and opinions — owes two. Paid: nothing.
Dr. Vetch, a bag — owes one. Paid: nothing.
The Alder twins — owe nothing, they pole under the causeway and say that is a matter of water, not land.
Old Serren, basket — owes one, and I have struck it out twice and put it back, because he always comes with the tide, and the tide has first call.

* * *

Reeve Holt — owes one. Came at an hour no reeve keeps and did not say what from.
Apprentice of the Guild, two lamps, oil — owes one, and no name in the line yet, because I do not know it, and because writing a name on a slate is a way of asking for it.

Nobody pays. That is the arrangement, and we are honest about it. When the road opens I will make a proper list, carry it to the reeve, and he will laugh, and we will be glad of the laughing.

At the bottom of every page, a lamp, chalked. When the slate is wet you find it with a thumb.

— Toll-Yann, booth, mid-causeway.
