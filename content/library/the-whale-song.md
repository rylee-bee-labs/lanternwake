Sung standing at dusk over the bones on the Boneyard beach. Old and slow. One voice holds the long note and the rest come in under it.

Slow down, slow down, the water is wide,
we swam it whole and we swim it yet.
Bone on the shingle, name on the tide,
nothing the sea has taken is the sea's to keep.

Slow down, slow down, the dark is long,
the deep gives back what the deep was sent.
Rib on the shingle, half a song,
sing it, and the other half is lent.

* * *

— Bonepicker's note, scratched into the back of the board:

I have sung it over Patience, and over Regret, and over a young one the storm left in two pieces. The words are the same every time. It is the wind that changes.

Stand at the ribs on an evening when the wind comes low and even out of the cave mouth. Sing the first line once. The ribs hum back. Not loud. A wooden sound under the note you are holding, like a second singer a little behind you.

They do it best after rain. They never do it at deep-night. I do not ask why.
