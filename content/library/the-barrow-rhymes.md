The rhymes I teach. Every visitor gets them. Learn them or do not, but you will hear them at your back the whole way down.

DOWN IS DARK AND DOWN IS LONG,
DOWN IS WHERE THE ROAD WAS FIRST.

UP IS MORNING, UP IS BREAD,
UP IS EVERY WORD YOU SAID.

Footnote: the first pair is said at the head of the stair, facing down, before anyone takes a step. Nobody is made to say it. Most do.

Footnote: the second pair is said at the top, going up, so the living hear their own words come back to them.

* * *

THE ROAD IS KEPT, THE ROAD IS FREE,
THE ROAD TAKES NOTHING, LET IT BE.

COLD STONE UNDER, CANDLE OVER,
EVERY MILE IS SOMEONE'S.

Footnote: the dead keep the road. That is the whole of our teaching. They charge nothing and they complain less than the living.

Footnote: "candle over" — the deep road is lit, one candle to the yard, by a hand nobody has seen. We do not light them. We only see they are not stepped on. If you find one out, leave it out. The one who lights them comes round again.

* * *

THE CANDLE IS NOT YOURS, THE CANDLE IS NOT YOURS,
LIGHT IT ANYWAY, THE ROAD IS OURS.

Footnote: children make that pair up themselves, roughly, and we let them. It is the only one with a mistake in it and it is the truest.

Footnote on the custom of the road: you may pass at any hour; nobody will ask your business. Heavy loads go down at the turns, not in the middle. Meeting someone, stand to the right and say your name. Saying your name down here is not a boast. It is a claim, and the dark has no claim on a name it has heard.

Footnote: say yours twice if you are afraid. Nobody here will laugh.
