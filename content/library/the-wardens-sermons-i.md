SERMONS OF THE FIRST DIM — spoken at the Drowned Chapel. Kept by F. Drennan, Gullwatch.

In the Warden's own hand. Borrowed and not returned: he would not have lent them, so I did not ask.

I keep them because they are true as words, every one, and because being true as words is not the same as being right.

My own marks are signed — D. His are not marked. He marked nothing that was his.

* * *

SERMON ONE — ON THE DIMMING OF THE LIGHT

...the light dims not because the dark is strong but because we have loved it too little. Every lamp we set was a small pride, a flame on a hill told it was ours. What we are given now is a mercy: an honest sea, which asks nothing of us and promises nothing.

Margin, my hand: Osric — the marsh candles did not ask to be lit either, and the marsh is warmer for them. D.

Margin, further down: "loved it too little." I have watched this coast carry oil up a cliff in a gale. We loved it exactly enough. We only got tired. D.

* * *

SERMON TWO — ON THE NAMES WE GIVE THE DARK

...let the dark have the far places. Let the road be nameless and the barrow nameless, and see how lightly we walk when nothing is ours to lose. A name is a chain, a chain is a weight, and the tide at last is teaching us to swim unburdened.

Margin: my mother's name is a chain I would carry up that cliff gladly. D.

Margin: he wrote this the week the weald lamp went out. I did not say so then. It is said here. D.

Margin at the foot: it is beautiful. God help us, it is beautiful, and it is wrong, and he is my friend. D.

* * *

SERMON THREE — ON WHAT WE OWE THE NIGHT

...and if one lamp must go down so that the coast may learn its own small worth, then let it be the dearest lamp, let it be —

Margin: the page stops here. He sat long over this one.

Margin: ten years I have read this leaf and never found the next line. He could not write it and did not preach it. I have stopped asking. D.

Margin, last: he is not a wicked man. He made one dark hour into a rule and obeyed it. When the lamps stand again I will give him back his sermons, and we will read them by a fire and laugh. D.
