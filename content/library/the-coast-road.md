Start at Thornhollow, the hollow: a town in a dip, which the wind goes over and leaves alone. Take water. There is a well, an inn, and a reeve who would rather hear your business from you than not.

The road runs out to Toll Rock and the causeway: stone laid across the brine, half drowned at high water. Cross at the ebb. The toll is a tin lantern and a word; the toll-keeper lights his own and has never lost one.

Beyond is Saltreach: marsh, salt pans, huts on stilts, and the Stilt Walk out to the ferry slip. Sing if you like. The marsh does not mind, and some say it prefers it.

* * *

From the marsh the road climbs north-east to the Boneyard: a beach of whale bones where the sea gives back what it takes, slowly. Camping is free. The bonepicker is there and will name your camp; let him.

Then Gullwatch, the cliff town: chandlery, ropewalk, chapel, headland. Buy rope. Walk the cliff path at the ebb only.

South into the Weald — black pine for miles with a clearing cut in the middle. The woodward keeps the way open; the trees keep one eye open. Do not leave marked ground at deep-night.

West is Barrow Mouth: a stone on a hill, a stair going down, a keeper who talks in pairs. The dead keep the road. They charge nothing.

* * *

Below Barrow Mouth the road is the deep road — a passage cut under the hill, candle-lit by hands unknown — and it comes up in the Drowned Chapel. The nave stands in salt water to the pillar bases. Take the east aisle; the west has gone soft.

Last is Vesper Point and the Beacon: the keeper's house, the great glass, the end of the ring.

Four days at an easy pace. Five if you talk to everyone along the way, which is the better way.

The road is a ring. You cannot get lost on a ring. You can only be on the wrong side of it, and the wrong side is one long walk from the right side.
