Names the dark has taken from this coast. Kept by Ash of the third hut. If you find this, read them out. A name said aloud is a name still held somewhere.

First winter:
Tobias Wren, marsh edge, at dusk. Read aloud by me at first light.
Little Hesta, the reed-cutters' girl. I gave her a second name when the first went: Gull. I use both.
A man off a boat, no name offered and none taken. I set him down here as Boatman and hope he does not mind.

* * *

Year four: the lamp-lighters of the weald road, one after another — Pell, his brother Ives, and Denner who kept the kiln. I read all three out under the pines and the wood said nothing.

Year six: a dog. I know. I put it down because the children asked where it was, and I would not leave them with an unanswered question.

Year nine: Corwin Thale, Master Lampwright, the chapel steps, the night the bell went. He is not gone the way the others went. He is only emptied. If you meet him, say his name to him and watch his hands.

* * *

And first, before all the rest, though I keep it last so it is read last:

Maren Ash. My sister. Taken at the third hut while I was at the pans. She came back the week after to borrow salt, and smiled at me like a neighbour.

Maren Ash

Underlined twice, twice a year, every year since. The dark may have her name. It will not have the ink.

Whoever reads this: read hers last, and read it twice.
