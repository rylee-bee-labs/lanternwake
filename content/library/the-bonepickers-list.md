The sea gave back, and I wrote it down, same as it comes. Eleventh of June — a boot, right foot, salt-eaten through. Nineteenth of June — a gannet, sound but for one wing. Second of July — a rope's end, knotted in a lover's hitch. Sixteenth of July — a boat's name-board, GARLAND, the paint gone to a ghost of itself. Third of August — an otter, asleep on the shingle, unbothered by me at all. Twentieth of August — a whistle, tin, still working. Second of September — a man's ring, two letters worn off it. The sea gives back everything it takes. It does not hurry and it does not say sorry.

* * *

Fifteenth of September — a rib, whale, which I have called Patience. Ninth of October — a lantern, glass whole, no wick in it. Twenty-eighth of October — a child's shoe. I wrote nothing else that day. Everything after it is in the same hand, and the hand steadier than it had any right to be. The last line of this book is dated ten years back and it is empty, and the tide has been very quiet about it ever since. I leave it open on the rock. The sea has not finished.
