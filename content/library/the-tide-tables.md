Twenty years in one hand. High water written large, low water small beneath it, spring and neap each carrying a ring. In the margins I have put the sayings where they came from and who gave them to me, so nobody has to take my word for aught: sky says quiet, water says later. Red sky at the mast, rope on the rack. Gulls sitting flat on the water — blow before the milk is skimmed. A westerly that smells of the marsh means the causeway walks under before dusk. Most of them are half true and every one has been useful, which is a different thing entirely.

* * *

Here. The ninth of the ninth month, ten years back. High water at the chapel stair, and in the margin beside it I have written: higher than the stone, higher than my father's flood of fifty-one, and nobody in three towns will dispute me. The equinox came that night with the wind behind it, and everything the sea did, it did at once. I have turned this date over so many times the paper has gone soft at the fold. The saying I would put beside it I have never written in the margin, because sayings are for weather, and what came that night was not weather.

* * *

Three pages after this are blank. I could not write figures for a year, and I have left the hole rather than go back and fill it, for a table with a lie in it is worse than a table with a hole. The last hand in this book is a child's — that rascal Perrin Nix — who copied out a whole month to see how it was done and put a drawing of a fish where the neaps should be. I have left the fish. It is as accurate as some of my springs, and a good deal better drawn.
