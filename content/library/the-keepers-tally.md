One candle a night, carried down. IIII IIII IIII IIII IIII. Four hundred nights counted at the head of the stair, and four hundred times the road came back empty, which is not the same as saying nothing took it. The candles go out one at a time at the turn. I have walked to the turn. The dark there does not move, and it does not smell of anything, and it has never once come up the steps to meet me. IIII IIII IIII IIII IIII. I count because counting is what is left when talking has stopped.

* * *

The total is the trouble. Ten years is three thousand six hundred and fifty nights, and the tally runs three thousand six hundred and fifty, and I have checked it twice a year with a stranger's eye over my shoulder. But the road is only four thousand paces, and a candle will not last four thousand paces in that wind. Ask any lampwright. So either the candles are longer than they look, or something down there is lighting them again, or the same candle has gone down more than once by somebody who lost the count. I do not write down which one I believe.
