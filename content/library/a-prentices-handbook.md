To be read once with a full lamp and once in the dark.

Five Great Lamps and one Beacon. The Causeway Lamp at Toll Rock, mid-span. The Ferry Lamp at the Saltreach slip. The Cliff Lamp on the Gullwatch headland. The Weald Lamp in the Ash Clearing. The Chapel Lamp in the drowned nave. The Beacon at Vesper Point sees them all and is not one of them; it is the coast's last word.

Ninety-nine flames once stood between the Beacon and the last candle in a marsh window. A prentice's work is the small ones as much as the great. The marsh window matters. It is somebody's supper.

* * *

The Evercan is the Guild's can and it is never empty. Carry it in the left hand, wick up. Oil from the Evercan lights what oil from a shop cannot. Ask Hobb Ferrant why and he will tell you he does not know, which is the Guild's oldest honest answer.

The ledger is the other heirloom. Write the lamp, the hour, the wind, and anything the lamp showed you. A lit lamp with no ledger is a kindness nobody can repeat. One line is enough. One line a night is a library.

What lamplighting is for: keeping places named. Light a road and it is a road. Leave it and it is ground that forgets it was walked on.

* * *

Traps, mending, weather, and the price of oil are in the back pages with the diagrams. Read them when you need them.

The rule the Guild never wrote down is this:

Show up.

Show up in the frost, when the wick is stiff and the road is a rumour. Show up when the lamp is already lit and there is nothing to do but trim it. Show up when nobody thanks you, which is most years. Show up on the night after the last one went out, when the dark has been waiting a whole season to be told it is not in charge.

Anybody can light a lamp. The Guild was only ever a promise to come back tomorrow.
