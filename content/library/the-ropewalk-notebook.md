Ropewalk notes — Halloran, Gullwatch.

TWIST. Three strands, never four. Three is a rope; four is a contest. You twist against the lay, and the rope comes together angry and strong, and after an hour it forgets it was ever separate.

LAY. Left hand, right hand, and the walk as long as the yard. A man's mood gets into hemp if he is not careful. I have made rope in a black mood and felt it under my palms the whole length. That one I unwound and made again.

SERVE. Wrap the ends before the sea finds them. Serving is politeness to a rope.

* * *

Every rope remembers the hands that made it.

Not mysticism. A plain fact of hemp. Where a thumb rode, the strands sit a hair flatter. Where a man hurried, the lay runs loose, and you can feel the hurry in a dark room with your eyes shut. Rope made in company is easy to spot: it runs true, because two men cannot hurry at once.

I keep a coil of the coast road's lamp-rope here, ten years old, chewed to nothing by the weather. It was the last thing made the old way, and it remembers more names than the chapel does.

* * *

One rope I did not make and never will: the bell's.

Stranded, tarred, laid right-handed, walked out on this very walk by a hand I know only by the rope — steady, unhurried, a little proud. The grain has one place worn smooth where a fist wore it, year on year, and under that, a place worn smaller, that held on harder.

The sea has it now. Salt rope rots from the inside out and gives no warning. I will not sell you a lie: that rope is gone.

But it held. Whatever else is said about that night, the rope held, and it held because of how it was made. — H.
