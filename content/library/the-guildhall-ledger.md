Fourth day of the ninth month. Oil bought: eleven tuns from Vane's chandlery at Gullwatch, at the old price. Lamps trimmed on the coast road, Thornhollow to Toll Rock, ninety-nine accounted for and not one short. Wind out of the north-east. Glass cracked on the Causeway Lamp and mended the same day by Hobb's boy, who did not wait to be asked. Supper was bread and fish. The Master ate standing up and went out again with the lantern. Nothing was said. It was that kind of week.

* * *

Written after, in a hand that presses hard through the paper: the hall came down on the ninth night. A storm lamp hung on the beam above the accounts, and the beam was dry as old bone, and the wind came in under the door. That is the whole of it. No hand in it but the weather's. I have gone through the accounts twice and there is nothing else to find. Whoever reads this: the lamps were only ever oil and work. Read it twice.
