The toll book of the causeway. Kept since the road shut, because a toll-keeper with nothing to do still keeps.

Crossings, first winter. What they carried. What they paid.

Marta Brine's cart, salt and lamp oil: nothing. The Saltreach twins, marsh reeds: nothing. Quill the clerk, papers: nothing. The reeve's man, a sealed letter: nothing. The Bonepicker, whale bone on his own back: nothing.

I have taken coin four times in ten years. I gave it back three.

* * *

Year seven, a hard one:

Ferrylight Oku, wet through, singing: nothing. A woman with a child and no name for either: nothing, and a lamp to the waterline for them. Hobb Ferrant, iron, walking it himself: nothing. Aisling Marr's nets: nothing. Old Serren, a basket and the tide at his heels: nothing.

The margin column is mine, not the reeve's. Every crossing I made with my tin lanterns lit, I draw one small lamp beside the row. The rule is mine: lamps up, road open, and the drawings are the toll.

* * *

Last winter's page:

Dr. Vetch, a bag, in a hurry: nothing. The reeve, alone, at an hour no reeve keeps: nothing. Two boys racing with no business out at all: nothing, and told to go home.

Ten years of nothing. It would look like a poor book to a stranger.

The lamps run down the edges of every page now. When the road stands open again I will start taking coin. Until then the drawings are the toll, and I am the one paying.
