Kept at the Beacon house, Vesper Point.

The Beacon's glass is ground, not cast. Blank first, then the rough wheel, then the sad-stone, then the wooden wheel with rouge and a wet hand. A grinder who hurries clouds the pane and is turned away without a word.

Trimming: the edge is taken down until the pane rings true under a knuckle, then bedded in putty soft as cheese and pinned with brass. Too hard, and the winter finds the seam by Christmas.

Keeping: wash with salt water and a clean rag. A thumb's grease burns into a haze the first time the lamp is lit, and a haze in a great glass throws a shadow the size of a boat.

* * *

For anyone who must light a lamp in a hurry — on a wet step, in a wind, in the dark — the guide gives three lines and no more.

Pour slowly. The Evercan is heavier than it looks, and oil finds its own way down; a splash in the fount is a flare in the face.

Strike once. Strike it dry, cup it in the hand, put it to the wick before it smokes. A second strike is smoke. A third is a wasted match.

Stand back. Let the flame take the glass on its own terms, give it one breath, trim, and go.

Three lines, written large, nailed by the door. Every keeper since has kept the nail.
