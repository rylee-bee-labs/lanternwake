Candle for the living, candle for the name.
Leave one on the stair and one in the flame.

*Footnote: said at the top of the Under-Stair by the Barrow-keeper — one line to a traveller going down, one to whatever is coming up. Travellers repeat only the first half. That is thought rude here.*

* * *

Down is dark and down is long,
down is where the road was first.

*Footnote: said going down, always twice — once for the step and once for the dark. Children of Barrow Mouth learn it before they learn to count.*

Cold stone, warm candle, keep your hand on the wall;
the dead went this way and they left it tall.

*Footnote: sung by the keeper when the passage drips. The last word is a lie — the road is not tall — but it keeps people from stooping, and stooping is how heads get cut.*

* * *

One for the road and one for the road's end.
Carry the light with you; send it on ahead.

*Footnote: said at the last landing, where the deep road meets the drowned chapel's undercroft. Whoever says it lights a candle and does not look back at it. The next pair comes from the chapel side and is not written here.*

Up is morning and up is bread.
The road keeps what the road is owed.

*Footnote: said coming up, by whoever reaches air first. If a party loses one, the survivors say it for them at the top. That is why the rhyme never changes and never gets shorter.*
