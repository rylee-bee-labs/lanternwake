I have written it down at last so that it is not only in me. The lamps went up one by one down the coast, and each year there were more of them, and I began to hear them the way you hear a guest who has taken off his boots and means to stay. Ninety-nine flames, and a man counting them. I told myself the Hush was judgment and the light was the thing being judged, and I said it in the nave to people who wanted something to be true. It was a comfortable sentence. I kept it warm.

* * *

The equinox. Wind like a gate off its hinges. I went up the stair with the rope-knife and told myself I was ending a false light, and the bell was not even a light, it was only a bell that the children liked. The sea was already standing in the aisles. I cut it, and it went down without a sound worth the name, and Corwin came running with a lantern in his fist and was not in time. He went in after it. He was shouting something and the wind took it off him. That is the whole of it. I have no other account to give.

* * *

Orra — I have written you eleven times. Your brother is not dead. He is here, and I am the reason he is not himself, and I could not make my hand say it. Drennan — you prayed for me all one winter and I let you, and I knew. Hobb — you forged the knife, you did not make me lift it. And the children who come up the stair at dusk to ring and find nothing there: I have no letter for them at all, and that is the worst page in this chest.

* * *

Tonight I set them out in order and I find that not one of them is sent. I keep them, I think, as a punishment, though I have stopped being sure the sea agrees I have earned it. If you are reading this in my chest, then somebody has opened what I kept shut, and that is a mercy I would not have granted myself. Take the seal. Give it to whoever is counting the lamps now. I am sorry I was the one who stopped them.
