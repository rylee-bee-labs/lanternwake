Household book — the keeper's house, Vesper Point. Kept by Lise Alder. Not a log of the light; the light mostly keeps itself. A log of the house that waits under it.

MENDS, TEN YEARS: the north shutter, twice. The kettle's handle, soldered by Hobb and derided by everyone. The stair tread at the seventh step, done properly rather than the way my father did it. The door that swells in the wet and has to be lifted and dropped, written down here so a stranger can do it. Two windows re-leaded. The pump. The pump again.

* * *

WHAT IS KEPT IN THE HOUSE: the Beacon's tools, oiled. A cask of oil in the dry corner, filled to the mark whether or not the light is standing. Bread, most days. The kettle on from first light, so no hour finds it cold.

And one extra cup, on the shelf, with the plain ones.

It has been out ten years. I do not put it away. Putting it away is a decision, and I have not made it.

Repairs are not hope. Repairs are repairs. But the house holds heat better every year I mend it, and a house that holds heat is ready for a wet coat through the door.

Whoever comes home comes home to heat.
