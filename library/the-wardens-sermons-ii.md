Sermons II — Osric Vane, Warden of the Drowned Chapel. Kept in the vestry chest, in the Warden's hand.

SERMON V — ON LIGHT.

The first book of these was sure of nearly everything. This one is not, and I will not pretend otherwise from this rail.

I have said the lamps are pride, and I say it still. But I have watched a lit window hold a name through a whole winter, and the same window go dark and the name with it. A man who ignores what he watches is not steady. He is only loud.

I write slower than I used to. The words take longer to find, and I have started leaving some out.

* * *

SERMON VI — ON JUDGMENT.

Three lines. The Hush pools where we do not go. We do not go where the lamps are out. Draw your own line; mine was wrong before.

SERMON VII — ON THE BELL.

The bell was holy. I hear another telling, with my voice in it. I will not write it down. Not here.

SERMON VIII — ON WATCHING.

I walk the nave at deep-night. The sea stands in the aisles, very polite. I am so tired. Someone should tell me to sit.

Someone did, once, hood down in the doorway. I told him the dark was judgment. He went down the stair anyway. I have not written his name, and that is the sermon.

* * *

The last page. The hand is unsteady.

SERMON IX — ON ASKING.

Sea, give the bell back.

You took it at high water with the wind in the east. You were asked to.

I am asking now for the other thing. Give the bell back. It was not mine to cut, and the water is too wide for a man to be right in.

I will say it at dusk in the empty nave until one of us gives in.

Sea, give the bell back.

The page ends there. Along the margin, in smaller letters where the paper runs out: it is not the sea.
