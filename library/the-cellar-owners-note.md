If you are reading this under the Brine & Wick, you are standing in a Guild room. The inn was built over it, not instead of it. Look at the west wall, low, behind the barrels: the shelf marks are cut in the stone still. A lamp for each bay. Oil here, wicks there, glass wrapped in sacking at the end where the damp comes in. I kept the inn's potatoes in those bays for forty years and never once painted over the marks. They were here first, and they were here for the light.

* * *

When the hall burned they brought the spare stock down and left it. Half of it is mine now, I dare say, for keeping the roof up over it. The other half is not mine and I have never counted it as such. Take what you need and write it in the book by the stair. There is always a book by the stair on this coast. Mind the third step on your way out. It has been telling people that for a hundred years and nobody listens to it.
