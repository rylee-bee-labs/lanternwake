Shelf marks, old Guild store-room, decoded by T. Quill in pencil, on the back of a receipt. Pencil because ink is a waste down here, and because a clerk's mark should be reversible.

O with a bar through it — oil. The barrels stood on that shelf and the bar is where the chocks went.

W, three strokes and a tail — wicks. The third stroke is short: the clerk counted what was left, not what was owed.

R, backwards — rope. Backwards because the rope hangs, and the mark was cut to be read from the walk, not the wall.

* * *

W with a fleck — winter stores. Flour, tallow, salt, the sacks that keep a cellar from being only a hole.

And one mark with no key: a small square with three dots inside it, cut deeper than the others, near where the north wall used to go through.

I have worked every book the Guild left. No inventory marks a shelf that way. There is a gap in the Guildhall's own plan, a room the right size, drawn in after the second fire and never labelled.

The mark is for a room that no longer exists. I would like that to be the end of it. If you find the room, tell me before you tell the reeve.
