Stilt Walk, Saltreach marsh. Kept by whoever has the nails.

Year one of the Dim — three planks out past the second bend. Wren Alder felled the pine and carried it on her back. Sable brought the nails in her apron and grumbled the whole way across.

Year two — the handrail went down in a gale. Halloran walked over from Gullwatch and spliced it to the posts with his own rope. He would not take a coin. Rope likes a job, he said.

Year three — two posts leaned in the thaw. Toll-Yann gave the booth's old boards and Ferrylight Oku poled them out to the walk at dusk.

* * *

Year eight — Edric Thorne brought ash planks from the Weald and stayed to fit them himself. Grandmother Ash watched from the third hut and said nothing, which from her was praise.

Year ten — no storm and no thaw, but the walk had gone soft at the knees. Five children of the marsh dragged the new wood out on a sledge made of old wood.

Then one night the whole walk sang underfoot — every plank at once, one low note like a struck rope — and nobody on it. They mended nothing that night. They walked it twice, to be sure, and went home.
