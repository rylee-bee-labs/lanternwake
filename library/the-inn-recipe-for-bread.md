Bread, the Brine & Wick, for anyone who asks. Written down at last so the asking stops.

Four measures of flour, plain. One of salt. Water off the boil, warm as a wrist. Yeast from the last batch, kept in a jar of flour above the bread board. If you have none, ask at the door; somebody always has.

Mix until your arm says stop, then work it twice more. Set it by the fire under a cloth. It wants an hour, or two if the house is cool, or as long as your errands.

Knock it down, shape it, second rise at half the first. Bake hot, lid on twenty minutes, lid off till it knocks hollow.

* * *

The crust, if you want the crust: a dish of water on the oven floor.

The secret, which is what people mean by the secret:

You do not make it for the bread. You make it for whoever is going to eat it. You think about them while you work it — the one out on the road, the two not speaking, the one who will come in soaked and not say where they have been. The bread takes it up. Hobb says there is no such thing and eats four slices.

Make enough for the one who has not arrived yet.

— Marta Brine. Ask at the door. It is no trouble, and the asking is half of it.
