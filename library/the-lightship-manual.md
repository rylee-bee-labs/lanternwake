LIGHTSHIP MANUAL. Kept at the chandlery and lent to anyone going out in weather.

CHAPTER ONE — OIL.

A light is oil with a job. Check the cask before you check the sky. Warm the oil if the frost has been at it: cold oil crawls, and a crawling wick gutters. Never top a cask and call it full. Measure. The sea does not round up for you.

* * *

CHAPTER TWO — WICK.

Trim to the nail, not to the eye. A wick left long smokes, and smoke is a liar: it reads as a light from the water and is not one. A wick trimmed short burns low and honest. Snuff it at the top of the hour whether it wants it or not.

Glass: dry cloth, then salt. Spit fogs glass, and glass is the only window a light has.

CHAPTER THREE — PATIENCE.

Storms do not last and neither do you. Sit. Count the beats of the light instead of the minutes of the night: four on, four off, the way a lightship answers. The counting is not for the light. It is for the sitter.

* * *

CHAPTER FOUR — COMPANY.

Two keep a light better than one, and neither is braver alone. Take the watch in halves. Speak when you change over, even if it is only "still burning." A light kept in company keeps the keepers too.

CHAPTER FIVE — A LIGHT FOR SOMEONE WHO IS NOT COMING BACK.

Keep it anyway. You will hear the arithmetic and you will agree with it at dawn. Light it at dusk. It costs a thumb of wick and a mouthful of oil.

The light does not fetch anyone. It says one thing, to the water and to yourself: this house is still a house. I have kept one ten years. The kettle goes on with it.
