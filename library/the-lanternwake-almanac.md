For the shelf of anyone who keeps one.

Tides. Two a day, high at the turn of the moon, and the coast reads them the way other places read a clock. The causeway has drowned at high water since before the road was a road. The nave floods to the pillar bases and stands there a while, patient. Spring tides take the shingle off the Boneyard and put it back changed.

Towns, five, strung on the ring: Thornhollow in the hollow; Saltreach on the marsh; Gullwatch on the cliff; Barrow Mouth on the hill; Vesper Point at the end of the world, or so the children say. The drowned chapel stands between two of them and belongs to none.

* * *

Trades: net-mending, rope-walk, chandlery, smith, toll, ferry, inn, clerk, woodward, tide-reading, bone-picking, lamp-lighting. The last is called a trade and is closer to a vow.

Lamps: five Great Lamps and the Beacon, and ninety-nine small ones counting down to a marsh window. Names are kept the way oil is kept — by measure, by hand, by whoever is left.

Who lives where is a page of its own and turns every season. The innkeeper and the smith at Thornhollow; the ferry-twins and the marsh hermit at Saltreach; the chandlery, the ropewalk and the priest at Gullwatch; the keeper of the stair at Barrow Mouth; the keeper of the house at the Beacon.

* * *

What the Hush is — nobody agrees, and the almanac will not be the first to pretend otherwise.

It is judgment: Sister Ilva, Toll-Yann, Father Drennan.
It is weather: Dr. Vetch, and he counts it like weather.
It is forgetting that goes all the way down: Grandmother Ash, who has been inside it.
It is the dark pooling where no lamp has been in a while: the Guild's old answer, kept plain.

Agreed: it does not kill. It takes names, then shapes, then reasons. Things left in it come back light, or do not come back. People who come out of it come back kind and empty, which the coast has decided to meet with supper.

* * *

The fold-out: two feet of paper and the whole coast, written on it.

From Thornhollow the road runs east and down to Toll Rock and the causeway; across the brine to Saltreach — marsh, boardwalk, the ferry slip; north-east to the Boneyard beach and its bones; up the cliff to Gullwatch; south into the Weald, whose pines lean to a clearing called ash; west to Barrow Mouth and the stair; down the deep road under the hill; up into the Drowned Chapel, which has a roof and no floor; north again along the headland to Vesper Point and the Beacon.

It is a ring. Fold it where the ink is thickest and you can hold the coast in one hand.
