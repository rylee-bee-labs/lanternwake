Crows of the Weald — observations, kept by E. Thorne, woodward.

They carry small bright things to the barrow: a button, a coin, a fish's eye, the little glass charms off a lantern frame. They take them from hedgerows and shingle and doorsteps, and they go south-west with them, into the dark under the hill, and they will not say whose they are.

Ask a crow whose it was and it looks at you the way a clerk looks at a bad sum.

I have stopped asking. I have started noticing which doorsteps they take from. That was worse.

* * *

The trees lean toward the lamp clearing and the crows lean toward the barrow, and both keep accounts I am not let to read.

Advice for walking the Weald after dusk, learned the long way:

Carry a light and keep it hooded until you need it. A lit thing in the dark is a question, and the wood will answer.

Walk the old charcoal path, not the short one.

Breathe out loud, so the wood knows you are not something waiting. Do not whistle a tune — crows hate a tune and will follow it.

If the crows go quiet all at once, stand still and let them pass over you. They are carrying something, and it is not yours.
