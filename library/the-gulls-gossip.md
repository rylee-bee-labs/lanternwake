---
title: The Gulls' Gossip
found: resident
speaker: skua
kind: note
---
Things the gulls have told me, which is to say shouted over my head and left there.

Where the fish run: the gut off the north shingle, three days after a west wind, at the turn of the tide. Sable knows it too and will not say, which is fine. A secret shared with a bird is still a secret.

When the storm comes: gulls come in at dusk in threes. Never fours. Threes means weather before morning. Fours would mean something else, and we have not had fours in ten years, and I do not care to think on what fours would mean.

* * *

One more, and this one is not a joke, so mind it.

On the deep road, past the barrow, there walks a man with no name. The gulls will not say whose he was. When I ask, they go quiet in the manner of birds who were told something in the nest and were not meant to repeat it.

He carries a candle at his hip and lights the road with it, one a night, and stands a while by each one. The gulls follow him as far as the pines and no farther.

Walk in the dark if you like. But when a gull will not go past a place, I take the gull's view.
