This is my book of songs. Ferrylight Oku. I rite them down becose when I for get them the water has them.

THE POLE SONG
Pole and pull, pole and slow,
water keeps what it don't know.
Pole and pull, pole and near,
first light is the next thing here.

Wren says fix the speling. I say the song woud go flat.

* * *

A MARSH SONG (Sable says I sing too much. She sings too much.)

Reeds gold, reeds grey, sing the dark away.
Pan and pail and a slow cold trail,
sing it any way.

Owt to owt and the mist come down,
you are not lost, you are only in the reeds.

That last bit is not a rime. Sable says it is. I think it is just true.

I keep the wrom wunspellings. They are the tune.

* * *

THE BELL UNDER THE WATER — not mine. Nobody knows whose.

Bell in the bay, bell below,
ring for the ones who do not go.
Bell in the bay, bell deep,
ring us the road while we sleep.

Bell in the bay, bell dumb,
ring anyway, the tide will come.

Grandfather sang it before me and his grandmother before him. He says do not put a tune on it that the bell did not ask for. So I sing it flat, like he did, and the water comes up a little when I do. That is all.
