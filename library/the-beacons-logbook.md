Vesper Point, kept by Lise Alder. Night of the storm: the glass went, the fire went, the rope is over the side and I have not counted it. The Master went down the road toward the chapel and has not come back. I have written DARK in this column every night since, because there was no other word to write. The first year I crossed it out at every dawn, on the chance the morning would argue with me. It never did.

* * *

The petition runs four years deep. Toll-Yann wants it lit so the causeway keeps its name. Orra wants it lit so the lightship crews can take a bearing. Father Drennan wants it lit for the bell's sake, which he would not say aloud if you paid him. Grandmother Ash wrote one line — LIGHT IT — and posted it from the marsh, eighty miles, twice. A boy ran up from Thornhollow to ask in person and could not get the words out for breath, so I gave him tea and wrote the words down for him. I have never had to throw a page away.

* * *

Deep-night is the worst hour up here. From the house step you can see the whole coast, and there is nothing on it but other people's windows, and every one of those is somebody holding a candle in two hands like a bowl of water. The Hush stands out in the water and takes no notice of me. It has never come up the hill. I think the hill is waiting for something and it knows it. I keep the kettle on, the tools greased, the log written. A keeper who has stopped writing has stopped being a keeper, and I am not ready to be that yet.

* * *

Whoever you are: the lens wants no oil, only clean glass and a steady hand. The winding key is on its nail by the door. Take the stairs two at a time — the third tread is a liar, as it always has been — and stand out at the gallery before you strike, so you can see the road. It is a long road and it has been dark a while, and the towns will not believe it at first; they will come out and stand in it with their faces up. Mind the wind off the rail. Put the log on the sill where the light can fall on it. Then come down and have the tea. It is still hot.
