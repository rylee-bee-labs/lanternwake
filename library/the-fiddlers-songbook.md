Fiddle book. My shorthand, so only I can read it, and half of that on a good night.

THE LAMP-LIGHTING REEL. Reel-time, four to the bar. Open A drone under everything the whole way. Bow: long-short, long-short, like a man on stairs.

First figure walks up the low string, second answers off the E. The middle eight is the halt — two beats where nobody plays at all, which is the moment the wick catches — then in, all together, and the room stands up.

Played every year for the lamplighters leaving, on the square.

* * *

THE DROWNED BELL LAMENT. Slow, and slower than you want. No drone. Flat of the bow the whole way, so it sounds like something carried and not something walking.

Four bars: the toll, low on the D string, always the same three notes and never the fourth. The fourth is the note the bell had, and the water has it now.

Four bars: the answer, up on the A, quieter each time.

Nobody plays the last line. It is written for a bell and a fiddle cannot be a bell. When we play it for the coast we stop before it and sit a while instead, and that is the last line.

* * *

MARSH LULLABY. Three notes and a hush. Sable sings it over at the huts now, and it is more hers than mine, which is a fine trade.

Time not marked. You follow the breath of whoever is sleeping. The figure is a fall: the tune steps down and down and never comes back up, which is what putting a child down feels like.

Keep it in the first position. Do not reach, do not stretch. A lullaby with a stretch in it wakes the child.

Second time quietly, third time hardly at all, then bow on the strings with no pressure, for wind in the reeds.

* * *

UNPLAYED. I have written its name at the top of this page and crossed it out eleven times, which is its own kind of name.

It was the Master's. Corwin Thale used to ask for it at the end of the night, and Hobb would put his mug down and say "not that one, Corwin," and Corwin would say "that one," and it went three rounds until Marta played it badly, on purpose, to end the argument.

Nobody plays it now. Not because it is sad. Because everyone remembers the words.

Learn it right. Play it somewhere the old ones cannot hear. Or where they can. I have had ten years to decide which is kinder and I have not.
