Of the clearing's name — Edric Thorne, woodward.

In the middle of it stands a lamp stump, cut low, black to the heart. The Weald Lamp stood in it within living memory. The glass is gone. The hook is gone. The stump stays, because a stump in living ground is slow to rot and quick to remember.

Before the lamp there was a fire here — a charcoal burn, older than the road and older than the chapel. The men who worked it left a ring of grey that no root would cross. The pines came back around it and would not step in.

So: a fire, and a lamp, and a clearing that has been ash twice over.

* * *

The woodward's rule, kept since the first lamp in this wood:

Every lamp is set in living wood. No stone plinths. No iron driven into rock. A post cut short and planted where it still bleeds resin and the branches still lean toward the flame.

Living wood keeps the lamp honest. A tree that carries a light will lean, and a leaning tells you things: when the flame is crooked, when the oil is low, when the wind has the wick. The Weald does not speak. It tilts.

Set your lamp inside the old stump's ring. Cut nothing. When it is lit again, the wood will know before you do.
